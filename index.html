The thesis

A name that states a promise.

ElderRoute.com does not sound like software. It sounds like a promise kept: an older person, a route that was planned, a driver who arrives, a door that opens, a chair that is waiting at the other end.

That is the right register for this market. The buyer of a senior-transport brand is not selling speed. The buyer is selling steadiness to people who have every reason to be wary of a missed pickup — and to the adult children, clinics, and plans who judge the service by the one trip that went wrong.

The name works across the whole field. A non-emergency medical transport fleet can run its dispatch board under it. A broker can coordinate trips under it. A health plan can put a member benefit under it. A senior-mobility startup can build an on-demand service under it. Each use reads the same way on first contact: this is the route for an elder, arranged with care.

Plain, and hard to confuse. Two everyday words, in the order a person would say them. No invented spelling, no jargon, no acronym to explain. It is easy to say on the phone to an 82-year-old, easy to print on the side of a van, and easy to remember after a clinic hands a patient a card.
